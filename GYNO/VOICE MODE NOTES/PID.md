PID REVIEW FLOWCHARTS
======================

DEFINITION AND PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
-------------------------------

VAGINA (unsterile)
  |
  v
CERVIX
  |
  v
ENDOMETRIUM
  |
  v
FALLOPIAN TUBE
  |
  v
PERITONEAL CAVITY

---

STAGE NAMES

cervix      -> cervicitis
endometrium -> endometritis
tube        -> salpingitis
ovary       -> oophoritis
tube+ovary  -> tubo-ovarian abscess
peritoneum  -> peritonitis



DEFENSES AND RISK FACTORS
--------------------------

NATURAL DEFENSES

vaginal pH ~4 (acidic)
  |
closed cervix
  |
mucous plug seals os
  |
IgA in cervical mucus
  |
intact hymen
  |
downward ciliary movement

---

WHEN DEFENSES BREAK DOWN

instrumentation (D&C, HSG,
  biopsy, abortion)
     |
     v
breach of cervix / mucous
plug

IUCD thread
     |
     v
allows ascent

broad-spectrum antibiotics
     |
     v
kill lactobacilli
     |
     v
pH rises
     |
     v
loss of acid barrier

STIs / multiple partners
immunosuppression (HIV)
     |
     v
direct infection risk



CLINICAL PICTURE
------------------

ACUTE PID

sudden onset
  |
  v
abdo pain, discharge,
fever, dyspareunia
  |
  v
exam: fever >38, tender/
rigid abdomen
  |
  v
bimanual: cervical motion
tenderness

---

CHRONIC PID

unresolved acute PID
  |
  v
ill, malnourished
  |
  v
chronic pain, back pain,
deep dyspareunia,
menorrhagia, subfertility

---

CHRONIC COMPLICATIONS

inflammation (repeated)
  |
  v
adhesions + fibrosis
  |
  v
tubal narrowing/occlusion
  |
  v
subfertility, chronic
pain, ectopic risk

---

PID vs ENDOMETRIOSIS

PID trigger
  = infection

ENDOMETRIOSIS trigger
  = menstruation

shared outcome
  = adhesions, pain,
    fibrosis, tubal
    occlusion, subfertility



CAUSES AND DIAGNOSIS
----------------------

MAIN ORGANISMS

most common:
  N. gonorrhoeae
  C. trachomatis

less common:
  Gardnerella vaginalis
  Mycoplasma hominis
  M. tuberculosis
  E. coli
  H. influenzae
  Group B Strep
  Pneumococci

---

DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS

PID mimics:
  appendicitis
  ectopic pregnancy
  twisted ovarian cyst
  septic abortion
  pyelonephritis

---

INVESTIGATIONS

blood: WBC up, CRP up
  (acute), ESR up (chronic)
     |
urine: rule out UTI
     |
PCR/NAAT: gonorrhea,
  chlamydia
     |
ultrasound: masses,
  abscess, free fluid
     |
     v
GOLD STANDARD: laparoscopy
  (direct view + sampling)



TREATMENT
-----------

IF SEPTIC

resuscitate first
  |
  v
IV fluids + analgesia
  |
  v
start broad-spectrum
antibiotics empirically
(do not wait for results)

---

ADMISSION DECISION

severe / sepsis / TOA /
failed outpatient tx /
unclear surgical dx
     |
     v
INPATIENT

mild, no sepsis
     |
     v
OUTPATIENT

---

OUTPATIENT REGIMEN

ceftriaxone 250-500mg IM
  single dose
     +
doxycycline 100mg PO BD
  x14 days
     +/-
metronidazole 500mg PO BD
  x14 days

alt: ofloxacin +
  metronidazole
chlamydia only: doxy
  7-14d or azithro 1g stat
gonorrhea only: ceftriaxone
  single dose

---

INPATIENT REGIMEN

ceftriaxone 1-2g IV daily
     +
doxycycline 100mg IV/PO BD
     +/-
metronidazole

beta-lactam allergy:
  clindamycin + gentamicin

---

SURGICAL OPTIONS

laparoscopy / laparotomy
  -> drainage, adhesiolysis

tubo-ovarian abscess
  -> US-guided aspiration

---

FOLLOW-UP

day 3
  -> check improvement

week 4-6
  -> check response,
     compliance, partner
     screening/treatment



HORMONAL AND ENDOMETRIOSIS NOTE
---------------------------------

PROGESTERONE EFFECT

inhibits proliferation
     +
maintains blood flow
     |
     v
no necrosis/shedding
     |
     v
no bleeding
     |
     v
no inflammation
     |
     v
fewer adhesions, less pain

---

ENDOMETRIOSIS THERAPY CHOICE

GnRH analogs
  -> effective
  -> limit: <=6 months
     (postmenopausal
     side effects)

danazol
  -> irreversible androgenic
     effects (hirsutism,
     voice change)
  -> used locally for deep
