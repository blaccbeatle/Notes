UTERINE RUPTURE VS DEHISCENCE
=============================


DEHISCENCE VS RUPTURE

Dehiscence
  |
  v
Partial myometrium separation
  |
  v
Serosa usually intact

---

Rupture
  |
  v
Myometrium + serosa separate
  |
  v
Uterine cavity open to
abdominal cavity

---

Dehiscence -> rupture
(rupture is the final event)



WHY FHR CHANGES COME FIRST

Uterus starts to separate
  |
  v
Endometrial receptivity and
myometrial function change
  |
  v
Placental blood flow falls
  |
  v
Fetal hypoxia

---

FHR pattern
  |
  v
Tachycardia first
  |
  v
Then bradycardia,
decelerations

---

Maternal signs come later
  |
  v
Blood loss builds slowly
  |
  v
750-1000 ml: tachycardia
  |
  v
1500 ml: hypotension



PAIN AND CONTRACTIONS

Labour pain
  |
  v
Contraction -> pain
  |
  v
Relaxation -> relief

---

Rupture pain
  |
  v
Continuous and severe
  |
  v
Pain in contractions AND
between contractions

---

Contractions
  |
  v
Normal: fundus -> downward
  |
  v
After rupture: integrity lost
  |
  v
No transmission -> contractions
cease



BLEEDING AND MATERNAL SIGNS

Rupture site bleeds
  |
  v
Blood collects in abdomen
  |
  v
Over about 1000 ml lost
  |
  v
Tachycardia, hypotension,
tachypnea, pallor
  |
  v
Hypovolemic shock

---

Extension to bladder
  |
  v
Stretched mucosa bleeds
  |
  v
Hematuria



CHANGES AFTER FULL RUPTURE

Baby extrudes into
abdominal cavity
  |
  v
Station goes up
(presenting part recedes)

---

Lie: longitudinal
  |
  v
Oblique / transverse

---

Presentation changes
(e.g. cephalic -> breech)

---

No uterine resistance
  |
  v
Fetal parts easily palpable

---

Blood mixes with liquor
  |
  v
Blood-stained liquor
