UTERINE RUPTURE VS DEHISCENCE
=============================

DEFINITIONS

scar under stress in labour
  |
  v
dehiscence
  - partial myometrium split
  - serosa usually intact
  - during contractions
  |
  v
rupture
  - myometrium + serosa split
  - cavity joins abdomen
  - final event

---

rupture follows the
dehiscence phase.



EARLIEST SIGN

uterus starts to separate
  |
  v
endometrium/myometrium
function altered
  |
  v
placental blood flow falls
  |
  v
fetal hypoxia
  |
  v
FHR abnormal = FIRST SIGN
  |
  +-> tachycardia first
  |
  +-> then bradycardia,
      decelerations

---

why maternal signs are late:

blood must accumulate
  |
  +-> ~750-1000 ml:
  |   tachycardia
  |
  +-> ~1500 ml:
      hypotension

---

detect early:

high-risk labour (prior scar)
  |
  v
continuous electronic
fetal monitoring



PAIN AND CONTRACTIONS

normal labour pain
  |
  v
comes and goes, relief
between contractions

---

rupture pain
  |
  v
continuous, severe,
no relief between
contractions

---

contractions after rupture:

wall integrity lost
  |
  v
wave can't cross the break
  |
  v
contractions weaken/stop



MATERNAL AND BLEEDING SIGNS

bleeding into abdomen
  |
  v
loss > ~1000 ml
  |
  v
hypovolemic shock
  - tachycardia
  - hypotension
  - tachypnoea
  - pallor

---

tear extends to bladder
  |
  v
hematuria



FETAL AND PRESENTATION CHANGES

baby extrudes into abdomen
  |
  +-> presenting part recedes
  |   (station goes up)
  |
  +-> lie/presentation lost
  |   (oblique/transverse)
  |
  +-> fetal parts easily
  |   palpable
  |
  +-> blood-stained liquor



ORDER OF SIGNS

1st  FHR abnormality
  |
2nd  continuous pain
  |
3rd  shock picture
  |
4th  contractions stop
  |
5th  bloody liquor
  |
6th  loss of lie
  |
7th  palpable parts
  |
8th  scar tenderness
  |
9th  presenting part recedes
  |
10th hematuria (maybe)

---

consequences of delay:

fetal hypoxia -> fetal death
  +
maternal hypovolemia
  |
  v
possible maternal death



MANAGEMENT

abnormal FHR
  |
  v
suspect rupture at once
  |
  v
emergency caesarean

---

aim: detect early, before
massive bleeding or shock.
