PERINEAL TEARS AND EPISIOTOMY
=============================



ANATOMY AND RISK

Perineal body (central tendon)
  |
  v
Where these meet:
  - bulbospongiosus
  - transverse perineal muscles
  - external anal sphincter (EAS)
  |
  v
Tissue stretched past limit
  |
  v
TEAR

---

RISK FACTORS

  Instrumental delivery
    (most modifiable)
  Macrosomia (>4 kg)
  Primiparity
  Shoulder dystocia
  Occipito-posterior position
  Prolonged 2nd stage
  Induction
  Asian ethnicity
  Midline episiotomy



PREVENTION

ANTENATAL
  |
  v
Perineal massage (late pregnancy)
Hydration
Nutrition

---

INTRAPARTUM
  |
  v
Warm compress (2nd stage)
  |
  v
Controlled head delivery
  |
  v
Hands-on OR hands-poised
at crowning

---

Mnemonic:
"Massage warmly, support hands"

MY ADDITION:
Warm compress and hands-on
both reduce severe tears.
Antenatal massage helps mainly
first-time mothers.



EPISIOTOMY

Deliberate incision to enlarge
introitus. NOT routine.
  |
  v
Indication present?
  - rigid perineum
  - anticipated tear
  - previous perineal tear
  - narrow outlet
  - forceps delivery
    (vacuum usually not needed)
  - fetal distress, quick
    delivery needed
  |
  v
Local anaesthetic
(2% lignocaine, about 10 mL)
  |
  v
Cut at crowning

---

MIDLINE
  |
  +-- Good: less blood, less
  |   pain, better healing,
  |   rare dehiscence, less
  |   dyspareunia, better scar
  |
  +-- Bad: higher sphincter
      extension, poor for
      manoeuvres

---

MEDIOLATERAL (about 60 deg)
  |
  +-- Good: low sphincter
  |   extension, better for
  |   manoeuvres
  |
  +-- Bad: more blood, pain,
      dehiscence, dyspareunia

---

MY ADDITION:
Restrictive use is current
practice. Routine episiotomy
does not reduce severe tears.



EPISIOTOMY REPAIR AND AFTERCARE

Repair in layers, inside out
  |
  v
1. Vaginal mucosa
   (continuous or locking)
  |
  v
2. Perineal muscles
  |
  v
3. Skin
  |
  v
Why layers?
No dead space, less haematoma
and infection, muscle realigned
  |
  v
Final checks:
  - swab, needle, instrument
    count
  - RECTAL EXAM (stitch in
    rectum? fistula risk)

---

AFTERCARE
  - hygiene
  - wash front to back
  - avoid scented products
  - avoid hot baths
  - no sex for 2 weeks
    (per notes)

MY ADDITION:
Most guidance now says resume
sex when comfortable, not by
a fixed date.

---

COMPLICATIONS
  |
  +-- Immediate:
  |   haematoma, infection,
  |   dehiscence,
  |   rectovaginal fistula
  |
  +-- Late:
      dyspareunia,
      scar tenderness



TEAR CLASSIFICATION AND MANAGEMENT

Perineal tear
  |
  v
1st degree
  vaginal mucosa + skin only
  |
  v
2nd degree
  perineal muscles involved
  sphincter SPARED
  |
  v
3rd degree
  anal sphincter involved
  |
  +-- 3A: <50% EAS torn
  +-- 3B: >50% EAS torn
  +-- 3C: EAS + IAS torn
  |
  v
4th degree
  EAS + IAS + rectal mucosa

---

ASSESSMENT
  |
  v
Consent
  |
  v
Good lighting
  |
  v
Rectal exam for EVERY tear
  |
  v
Senior input if 3rd or 4th

---

REPAIR ORDER
  |
  v
Analgesia
  |
  v
Rectal mucosa (4th degree)
  |
  v
IAS
  |
  v
EAS
(end-to-end or overlap)
  |
  v
Perineal muscles
  |
  v
Skin

---

MY ADDITION:
End-to-end and overlap give
similar outcomes. End-to-end
for partial EAS tears (3A,
some 3B). Either is fine for
full-thickness tears.

Also: single dose broad-
spectrum antibiotic, laxatives,
follow-up. Experienced operator
in theatre with good anaesthesia.



COMPLICATIONS OF SEVERE TEARS

Severe tear (3rd or 4th degree)
  |
  +-- Anal incontinence
  |   (pudendal nerve injury
  |   + sphincter damage)
  |
  +-- Rectovaginal fistula
  |
  +-- Haemorrhage, haematoma
  |
  +-- Infection, dehiscence
  |
  +-- Dyspareunia, scarring
  |
  +-- Psychological trauma
  |
  +-- Higher recurrence risk
      in later deliveries
      (counsel on mode of
      delivery next time)
