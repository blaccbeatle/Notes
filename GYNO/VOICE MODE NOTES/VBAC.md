===============



BASICS

VBAC = vaginal birth after
caesarean
  |
  v
Goal: fewer repeat CS,
fewer complications,
positive birth experience

---

Layers cut (outside to in):
  skin
   |
   v
  subcutaneous tissue
   |
   v
  rectus sheath
   |
   v
  peritoneum
   |
   v
  visceral peritoneum
   |
   v
  uterus
   |
   v
  amniotic membrane

Repair = reverse order:
uterus > peritoneum > rectus
sheath > fat > skin

---

Indications (common to less):
  1. fetal distress
  2. previous CS
  3. placenta previa
  4. lower segment fibroid
  5. heart disease / pulm HTN
  6. HIV (some cases)
  7. maternal request

Heart disease

TYPES AND COMPLICATIONS

Which incision?
  |
  +-- LOWER (preferred)
  |     forms after ~28 wks
  |     fibrous, few vessels
  |     less bleeding
  |     rupture ~0.5% (1:200)
  |
  +-- UPPER (classical)
        more muscle, bleeding
        rupture ~5% (10x)
        no VBAC after it

---

Upper segment used when:
  |
  +-- extreme emergency
  |
  +-- lower segment problem
        bladder adhesion
        lower fibroid
        previa low down

---

Complications:
  |
  +-- immediate
  |     bleeding, pain,
  |     anaesthesia, organ injury
  |
  +-- intermediate
  |     infection, endometriosis,
  |     repeat admissions
  |
  +-- long term
        hernia, higher risk
        with each repeat CS

---

placenta previa

  v

over scar
  |
  v
massive bleeding
  |
  v

Sri Lanka: limit ~3 CS

MODE OF DELIVERY

VBAC success
  |
  +-- overall: ~75%
  |
  +-- prior vaginal birth: ~90%

---

Favourable:
  prior vaginal birth
  non-recurring CS reason
    (distress, breech)
  younger age
  smaller baby
  head engaged
  spontaneous labour

---

Unfavourable:
  recurring CS reason
    (CPD, uterine anomaly)
  age 40+
  large baby
  induction

BENEFITS AND RISKS

VBAC
  |
  +-- benefits
  |     no surgery
  |     home next day
  |     future vaginal births
  |     less baby breathing
  |     problems
  |     no family size limit
  |     lower maternal morbidity
  |
  +-- risks
        rupture ~0.5%
        emergency CS
        instruments ~39%
        (vs 10-15%)
        sphincter damage, tears
        transfusion, bleeding
        endometritis

---

Baby's lungs:
labour stress
  |
  v
glucocorticoids
  |
  v
more surfactant
  |
  v
less respiratory distress

---

Repeat CS
  |
  +-- benefits
  |     planned date
  |     less transfusion
  |     less endometritis
  |     no scar rupture
  |     pelvic floor protected
  |     sterilisation option
  |
  +-- risks
        adhesions: bladder
        or bowel organ injury
        slow recovery
        more future CS, previa,
        adherent placenta, PPH
        wound infection
        spinal headache,
        meningitis

---

Why endometritis differs:
endometritis higher
after vaginal birth
  |
  v

CONTRAINDICATIONS

VBAC candidate?
  |
  +-- previous rupture?
  |     yes -> NO VBAC
  |
  +-- previous classical CS?
  |     yes -> NO VBAC
  |     (rupture risk 2-9%)
  |
  +-- 3 or more CS?
  |     yes -> NO VBAC
  |
  +-- previa or lower/cervical
  |   fibroid?
  |     yes -> NO VBAC
  |
  +-- none of these
        -> VBAC possible

---

Number of prior CS allowed:
  |
  +-- Sri Lanka: 1
  |
  +-- Western: 2
