INFECTIOUS DISEASES: RAPID REVIEW
=================================



1. TYPHOID (ENTERIC FEVER)

S. typhi        -> typhoid
S. paratyphi    -> paratyphoid (A,B,C)
Non-typhoidal   -> gastroenteritis

---

Faecal-oral (infective dose 10^5)
  |
  v
Incubation 5-21 days
  |
  v
Ileal Peyer's patches
  |
  v
Mesenteric lymph nodes
  |
  v
Primary bacteraemia
  |
  v
Seeds liver, GB, spleen, kidney, BM
  |
  v
Secondary bacteraemia

---

Fever + altered bowel habits
  -> always consider typhoid
Risk: unhygienic food/water,
      travel, food handlers

WEEK 1
  |- sustained high fever
  |- coated tongue, constipation
  |- BLOOD culture +
  v
WEEK 2
  |- step-ladder fever
  |- relative bradycardia
  |- rose spots (trunk/abdomen)
  |- soft splenomegaly
  |- diarrhoea may start
  |- STOOL culture +, Widal +
  v
WEEK 3
  |- recovery or complications
  |- haemorrhage
  |- perforation -> peritonitis
  |- URINE culture +

RIF pain     -> ddx appendicitis
FBC          -> can be normal
Other compl. -> encephalitis,
                meningitis, abscesses,
                myocarditis, carrier

---

INVESTIGATIONS
Blood culture   wk 1   most practical
                       (can be false -)
Stool culture   wk 2
Urine culture   wk 3
Bone marrow     any    MOST SENSITIVE
                       last resort
Widal (SAT)     wk 2+  poor sens/spec
                       rising titre

---

TREATMENT
1st line -> Ceftriaxone
Alt      -> Ciprofloxacin,
            Co-amoxiclav
AVOID    -> Chloramphenicol
            (resistance + BM suppr.)

---

CHRONIC CARRIER
Excretion > 1 year
  |
  +-- site: gallbladder (most common)
  |         or kidney
  +-- risk: females, biliary disease,
            bladder schistosomiasis
  +-- food handlers -> public health
      (Typhoid Mary)



2. TYPHUS (RICKETTSIOSES)

Scrub     Orientia tsutsugamushi
          -> trombiculid mite
Murine    Rickettsia typhi
          -> rat flea
Epidemic  Rickettsia prowazekii
          -> human body louse

---

Mite/flea moves on skin
  |
  v
Stops at tight areas
(undergarments, clothing folds)
  |
  v
Bite -> ESCHAR
  painless, brown, crusted
  "cigarette burn"
  VIRTUALLY DIAGNOSTIC
  + regional lymphadenopathy

---

Incubation ~2 weeks
  |
  v
High swinging fever, headache,
myalgia, red eyes, HSM, meningism
  |
  +-- LN: regional -> generalised
  |       (after 1st week)
  +-- rash: macular/maculopapular
  |         (trunk) -> petechial
  +-- NO prominent jaundice or
      renal failure (vs lepto)

Fever + rash      -> think typhus
Fever + deafness  -> think typhus
Fever + tinnitus  -> think typhus

---

COMPLICATIONS
Resp    -> pneumonia, effusion
Neuro   -> meningitis, encephalopathy,
           deafness, tinnitus
Vasculitis -> bleeding, multi-organ

---

DIAGNOSIS
Mainly clinical
FBC -> often normal, non-specific
       (leukopenia +/- neutrophilia,
        thrombocytopenia)
Weil-Felix -> low sens/spec
Immunoperoxidase / IF -> better
Antibody testing -> confirms

---

TREATMENT
Suspect typhus
  |
  v
DOXYCYCLINE x 10 days
  |
  v
Response in 1-2 days
  -> confirms diagnosis

Alt: chloramphenicol,
     fluoroquinolones
NO response: beta-lactams,
             aminoglycosides,
             ceftriaxone



3. DENGUE

4 serotypes (DEN-1 to DEN-4)
Vector: Aedes aegypti
  |
  v
1st infection -> type-specific
                 immunity
  |
  v
2nd infection (different serotype)
  |
  v
Antibody-dependent enhancement
  -> high viral load
  -> severe disease
  -> haemorrhage more common

Viraemia: first 3-5 days
  -> mosquito transmission risk

---

COURSE
Mosquito bite
  |
  v
FEBRILE PHASE (2-7 days)
same in DF and DHF
  |
  +--> DF  ----------> RECOVERY
  |
  +--> DHF --> CRITICAL --> RECOVERY
               (24-48 h)

DF and DHF = distinct courses
Mismanagement cannot turn DF -> DHF
DHF hallmark = LEAKAGE
               (not haemorrhage)

---

FEBRILE PHASE
|- high fever, facial flushing
|- headache, retro-orbital pain
|- arthralgia > myalgia
|- nausea, vomiting, mild bleeding
|- WBC < 5000
|- platelets < 150,000
|- lymphocyte predominance
|- TENDER HEPATOMEGALY
   -> warning sign, favours DHF

---

ADMISSION
Platelets < 100,000       -> ADMIT
Warning signs after day 3 -> ADMIT
  abdominal pain/tenderness
  persistent vomiting
  suspected leakage
  mucosal bleeding
  lethargy/restlessness
  liver > 2 cm
  rising HCT + falling platelets
Platelets 100,000-150,000
  + rapidly dropping   -> consider
Platelets > 100,000    -> repeat
                          next day

---

FEBRILE FLUIDS (>= 50 kg)
100 cc/hour (~2500 cc/day)
Give:  ORS, fruit juice, kanji, soup
Avoid: plain water (main fluid)
       red drinks
       NSAIDs, steroids
Fever: paracetamol only

---

CRITICAL PHASE (DHF)
Day 5-6, as fever settles
  |
  v
Capillary permeability up
  |
  v
SELECTIVE leakage
  -> pleural effusion
  -> ascites
  |
  v
Peak leak at 24 h
  -> highest shock risk

Generalised/periorbital oedema
  -> FLUID OVERLOAD (not leakage)
Gallbladder oedema
  -> monitor (not diagnostic)

---

CONFIRM LEAKAGE
1. HCT rise > 20%
   (baseline unknown:
    40% male / 36% female)
2. FAST scan (USG) -> BEST
3. Albumin < 3.5 g/dL
4. Non-fasting chol < 100 mg/dL

Not reliable: calcium,
              urine output,
              pulse pressure

---

ONSET ESTIMATE (FAST)
Pleural effusion only   -> ~6 h ago
Effusion + ascites      -> ~12 h ago
Larger effusion         -> earlier

---

PCV INTERPRETATION
Dehydration   -> PCV up ~10%
Leaking       -> PCV up ~20%
Shock         -> PCV up ~30%

Leak (PCV up) + bleed (PCV down)
  -> cancel out
  -> PCV looks NORMAL

Shock + normal/low PCV
  -> BLEEDING

---

CRITICAL-PHASE FLUIDS
Monitor:
  vitals hourly (PR, BP, CRT)
  I/O 3-hourly
  HCT 3-hourly
  catheter if high risk/shock
  |
  v
Give exactly what leaks
  |
  +-- less -> more leak -> shock
  +-- more -> overload in recovery
  |
  v
Urine output target
0.5-1 ml/kg/h
  |
  +-- < 0.5 -> leaking > given
  +-- > 1   -> giving > leaking
  |
  v
Rate: start ~1 ml/kg/h
      -> up to 7 at 24 h peak
      -> taper

---

FLUID QUOTA
Total (48 h) = Maintenance + 5%
Weight = lesser of ideal/actual
         MAX 50 kg
Maintenance = 100/50/20 ml/kg
5%          = 50 ml/kg
50 kg       -> 4600 ml

Example:
  0-20 h   1300 ml (stable)
  20-28 h  2500 ml (shock, boluses)
  at 28 h  3800 ml used
  left     800 ml for 20 h
Can slightly exceed if needed

---

SHOCK SIGNS
Symptoms:
  sweating, abdominal pain,
  vomiting, restlessness,
  postural dizziness,
  UO < 0.5 ml/kg/h
Signs:
  cold extremities, CRT > 2 s
  tachycardia, rising diastolic
  PP < 20 mmHg
  postural drop > 20 mmHg
  increased RR

---

SHOCK STAGES
Compensated   -> PP <= 20 mmHg
Decompensated -> SBP < 90, or
                 SBP fall > 30%, or
                 MAP < 60
Profound      -> BP/pulse
                 unrecordable

---

SHOCK MANAGEMENT
Compensated
  |
  v
O2 + urgent HCT
NS/Hartmann 10 ml/kg over 1 h
(max 500 ml)
  |
  v
Reassess at 1 h
  |
  +-- Improved (PP > 25, CRT < 2 s)
  |     -> 10-7-5-3-1.5 ml/kg/h
  |
  +-- Not improved -> check HCT
        |
        +-- HCT high
        |     -> 2nd crystalloid bolus
        |
        +-- HCT normal/low = bleeding
              -> PRBC 5 ml/kg
        |
        v
      Still not improved
        |
        v
      Correct ABCS
      (Acidosis, Bleeding,
       Calcium, Sugar)
        |
        v
      Group + cross-match FIRST
        |
        v
      Colloid
        Dextran 40 max 3/24 h
        Hetastarch max 5

Decompensated/profound
  -> rapid free-flow bolus until
     BP + pulse recordable
  -> then same protocol

Monitor: vitals + HCT every 15 min
         (before + after bolus)

---

BOLUS -> EXPECTED PCV FALL
500 ml NS        -> 3-4% (up to 5%)
250 ml dextran   -> 4-5%
5 ml/kg PRC      -> 5%
PCV falls 10%    -> suggests bleeding

Platelets < 10,000 alone
  -> NOT an indication for
     transfusion in DHF

---

RECOVERY (after 48 h)
Leaked fluid reabsorbed
  -> risk of FLUID OVERLOAD
Signs: well-being, appetite,
       bradycardia, stable HCT,
       diuresis
Rash: "white islands in red sea"
      +/- itching

---

CONFIRMATION
Day 1-5   -> NS1 antigen + PCR
After 6   -> IgM antibody



4. HIV

RNA retrovirus
HIV-1 -> most common worldwide
HIV-2 -> West Africa, less
         pathogenic, slower

---

Transmission
  sexual 0.005-0.5% per act
  blood, IVDU, perinatal,
  needle stick
  |
  v
gp120 binds CD4+ T cells
  |
  v
Replication -> CD4 destruction
  |
  v
Impaired immunity

---

STAGES
Acute -> often asymptomatic
  |
  v
Seroconversion (3-12 weeks)
  60-80% symptomatic
  glandular fever-like:
  sore throat, LN, rash,
  arthralgia, diarrhoea
  Antibody may be NEGATIVE
    -> HIV RNA PCR or p24
  Ddx: EBV, syphilis
  |
  v
Chronic/symptomatic
  fever, sweats, weight loss
  oral candida, hairy leukoplakia,
  zoster, seborrhoeic dermatitis
  |
  v
AIDS
  HIV + AIDS-defining illness
  OR CD4 < 200 cells/uL

Can mimic almost any picture
FBC -> pancytopenia, low lymphocytes
Fever + rash + LN + sore throat
  -> consider IM AND HIV

---

CD4 STAGING
Stage 1 -> > 500
Stage 2 -> 200-500
Stage 3 -> < 200
Clinical A/B/C
  (C = AIDS-defining)

---

AIDS-DEFINING
Cancers: Kaposi, Burkitt,
         primary CNS lymphoma,
         cervical cancer
Infections: PCP, TB, oesophageal
            candida, CMV, crypto,
            toxo, cryptosporidium,
            PML, MAC, chronic HSV
Other: HIV encephalopathy, wasting

---

DIAGNOSIS
4th-gen ELISA (Ab + p24)
  ~2 weeks
  |
  v
Western blot (confirm)
  |
  v
ELISA + / WB -
  -> repeat in 1 month
  -> still discordant = negative
Window period -> p24 or PCR

---

OPPORTUNISTIC
Lungs -> PCP -> cotrimoxazole
         (prophylaxis if CD4 < 200)
      -> TB (test all TB for HIV)
Eyes  -> CMV retinitis
         (fundus in PUO)
Gut   -> oesophageal candida
         CD4 < 100
         dysphagia/odynophagia
         -> fluconazole/itraconazole
CNS   -> toxo, crypto, PML
Skin  -> recurrent/multidermatomal
         zoster -> THINK HIV
Hepatitis + pneumonitis
  + diarrhoea -> CMV

---

TREATMENT
Start ART IMMEDIATELY
(do not wait for CD4)
  |
  v
2 NRTIs + (NNRTI or PI or INSTI)
NRTI:  zidovudine, lamivudine,
       abacavir, tenofovir
NNRTI: efavirenz
PI:    atazanavir
INSTI: dolutegravir
  |
  v
Monitor viral load + CD4
Normal life expectancy

---

PEP
High-risk exposure
  |
  v
Start within 6-8 h (max 72 h)
Duration 28 days
  |
  +-- low risk  -> 2 NRTIs
  +-- high risk -> 2 NRTIs
                   + boosted PI
  |
  v
Test at 6 wk, 3 mo (+/- 6 mo)

---

PREGNANCY
Caesarean preferred
Bottle feeding preferred
(SL: breastfeed if bottle unsafe)



5. INFECTIOUS MONONUCLEOSIS (EBV)

Adolescents / young adults
Incubation 4-8 weeks
  |
  v
Fever, malaise, sore throat
  |- palatal petechiae
  |- tonsillar enlargement
  |    -> airway risk (peds)
  |- POSTERIOR cervical LN
  |- splenomegaly -> rupture risk
  |- mild hepatitis +/- jaundice
  |- AIHA

---

Presumed strep -> amoxicillin
  |
  v
Transient red macular rash
(drug-related, not disease)

---

BLOOD
WBC UP (unlike most viruses)
Lymphocytes > 50%
Atypical lymphocytes >= 10%
  (CD8+ Downey cells)
Same pattern: CMV, toxoplasmosis

Dx: Monospot (Paul-Bunnell)
    or EBV IgM

---

COMPLICATIONS
splenic rupture, airway block,
haemolysis, thrombocytopenia,
myocarditis, meningitis,
encephalitis, mesenteric adenitis

Long term: Burkitt lymphoma,
           nasopharyngeal ca

---

MANAGEMENT
Self-limiting -> supportive
Steroids if:
  airway obstruction
  neuro complications
  marked thrombocytopenia
  haemolytic anaemia
No contact sports until
spleen normal



6. LEPTOSPIROSIS

L. interrogans  -> pathogenic
L. biflexa      -> non-pathogenic
Serovar icterohaemorrhagiae
  -> icterus + haemorrhage

---

Rodent (rats), cattle, dogs, pigs
  |
  v
Colonises renal tubules
(persists despite antibodies)
  |
  v
Shed in urine
  |
  v
Stagnant water (paddy, ditches)
  |
  v
Enters abraded skin / mucosa
  |
  v
Incubation 5-14 days

High risk: farmers, sewage/vet
           workers, river bathing
Human-to-human: rare

---

PHASE 1: BACTERAEMIC (week 1)
|- high fever
|- CONJUNCTIVAL SUFFUSION
|- headache
|- MYALGIA > arthralgia
|- CALF TENDERNESS
|- jaundice ~1/3 (Weil's)
|- NO lymphadenopathy
  |
  v
PHASE 2: IMMUNE (after week 1)
  antibody-mediated
  multi-organ damage

---

COMPLICATIONS
Renal   -> ATN, AIN, rhabdo
           K+ LOW (tubular loss)
Heart   -> early myocarditis
Lung    -> pulmonary haemorrhage
           (can be fatal)
CNS     -> aseptic meningitis
           (pyogenic rare)
Other   -> haemolysis, liver failure,
           acalculous cholecystitis,
           thrombocytopenia
NOT     -> pyelonephritis

Improving pt + arrhythmia
  -> hypokalaemia

---

LEPTO vs DENGUE
Lepto  -> myalgia > arthralgia
          calf tenderness
          conjunctival suffusion
Dengue -> arthralgia > myalgia
          leukopenia, lymphocytosis

LEPTO vs TYPHUS
LN        lepto absent / typhus +
WBC       lepto neutrophilia /
          typhus leukopenia
Platelets both low
Urine     lepto abnormal

---

INVESTIGATIONS
Week 1 -> Lepto-PCR (best early)
After day 7 -> MAT (GOLD STD)
  positive: >= 1:320 or 4-fold rise
  also IgM ELISA
FBC  -> neutrophil leukocytosis,
        low platelets
LFT  -> ALP up, AST > ALT
UFR  -> protein, blood, pus cells,
        granular casts, bilirubin
RFT  -> creatinine up, K+ low
CXR  -> infiltrates, haemorrhage
ECG  -> myocarditis

---

MANAGEMENT
Admit, 4-hourly temp, strict I/O
  |
  +-- severe -> IV CEFTRIAXONE
  |   alt: IV benzylpenicillin,
  |        IV cefotaxime
  +-- mild   -> oral doxycycline
  |
  v
Pulmonary haemorrhage
  -> IV methylprednisolone pulses
Renal failure
  -> correct electrolytes,
     dialysis if indicated
  -> dialysis drops K+ further
  -> ADD K+ TO DIALYSATE

---

PREVENTION
Vaccinate farm animals
Boots + protective clothing
Doxycycline 200 mg weekly
  (start before exposure)

Farmer + fever + calf pain
  + red eyes -> LEPTOSPIROSIS



7. MALARIA

Currently non-endemic in SL
Indigenous P. ovale reported
Travel (India, Africa) -> suspect
Fever + confusion + India travel
  -> malaria

---

Female Anopheles (definitive)
  |
  v
Sporozoites -> LIVER
(exoerythrocytic schizogony)
  |  hypnozoites (vivax/ovale)
  |  -> no symptoms
  v
Merozoites -> RBC
(erythrocytic schizogony)
  |  -> ALL symptoms
  v
Gametocytes -> mosquito

---

SPECIES
P. vivax / P. ovale
  benign tertian, every 48 h
  reticulocytes, enlarged RBC
  single parasite/RBC
  HYPNOZOITES -> relapse
  round gametocyte
  incubation 10-17 d
  vivax -> thrombocytopenia

P. falciparum
  malignant tertian, irregular
  any RBC, normal size
  MULTIPLE parasites/RBC
  no hypnozoites -> no relapse
  CRESCENT gametocyte
  incubation 7-14 d
  chloroquine-resistant
  worse in pregnancy

P. malariae
  quartan, every 72 h
  mature RBC
  incubation 18-40 d

P. knowlesi -> zoonotic

---

PAROXYSM
Cold (15-60 min) -> chills, rigors
  |
  v
Hot (2-6 h) -> high fever,
               headache, vomiting
  |
  v
Sweating -> temp falls

Signs: haemolysis, jaundice, HSM
NO rash, NO lymphadenopathy

---

SEVERE (falciparum)
|- cerebral (coma, seizures)
|    -> SEQUESTRATION
|    -> full recovery in majority
|- HYPOGLYCAEMIA
|- lactic/metabolic acidosis
|- ARDS / pulmonary oedema
|- AKI
|- blackwater fever
|- severe anaemia, jaundice
|- DIC (bad sign)
|- very high ESR

Fever + jaundice + anaemia
  -> falciparum
Fever + hypoglycaemia
  -> falciparum

---

RENAL
Falciparum -> ATN / interstitial
              -> AKI, oliguria,
                 blackwater
Malariae   -> membranoproliferative
              GN -> NEPHROTIC

---

DIAGNOSIS
Thick film -> detects parasite
Thin film  -> species
RDT        -> antigen
Always rule out mixed infection

---

TREATMENT
Confirm species
  |
  +-- P. vivax (uncomplicated)
  |     |
  |     v
  |   Chloroquine x 3 days
  |     |
  |     v
  |   CHECK G6PD
  |     |
  |     v
  |   Primaquine x 14 days
  |   (hypnozoites + gametocytes)
  |
  +-- P. falciparum
        |
        +-- uncomplicated
        |     -> ACT
        |     -> single-dose
        |        primaquine
        |        (gametocytes)
        |
        +-- severe
              -> IV ARTESUNATE
              -> oral ACT when stable
              -> single-dose
                 primaquine

ACT -> avoid 1st trimester
       safe later



8. MENINGITIS

Suspected meningococcal
  |
  v
Blood culture IMMEDIATELY
  |
  v
Dexamethasone at/before 1st dose
  |
  v
IV ceftriaxone / cefotaxime
  |
  v
LP later (never delay abx)

---

  -> 3rd-gen ceph + IV acyclovir
Bacterial likely -> omit acyclovir

Adjustments
  resistant pneumococcus
    -> add VANCOMYCIN
  > 50 yrs (Listeria)
    -> add AMPICILLIN

Bugs: N. meningitidis,
      S. pneumoniae, Hib

---

           sugar very low
           lymphocytes

           sugar normal/mild down
           lymphocytes

           sugar low
           neutrophils
Partially  protein high

---

TB MENINGITIS

9. SEPSIS

Suspected sepsis
  |
  v
Cultures ASAP
  |
  v
IV antibiotics
(never withhold for results)
  |
  v
IV normal saline
  |
  v
BP not improved
  -> NORADRENALINE (choice)
  |
  v
Still not improved
  -> VASOPRESSIN

---

MONITORING

  -> hypoxia, hypercarbia
  -> metabolic acidosis

ADJUNCTS

Distributive shock
Risk: extremes of age

17. HIGH-YIELD POINTS

Typhoid
  blood culture wk 1
  bone marrow most sensitive
  ceftriaxone

Typhus
  eschar -> doxycycline
  no response to ceftriaxone

Dengue
  hallmark = leakage
  HCT rise > 20%, FAST best
  4600 ml / 50 kg, max 50 kg
  decompensated: SBP < 90,
    fall > 30%, MAP < 60
  shock + low PCV -> bleeding
    -> PRBC 5 ml/kg

Leptospirosis
  suffusion + calf tenderness
  no LN, LOW K+
  MAT >= 1:320

Malaria
  vivax -> primaquine 14 d + G6PD
  falciparum -> ACT
  severe -> IV artesunate
  malariae -> nephrotic

HIV
  AIDS = CD4 < 200
  ART immediately
  PEP <= 72 h x 28 days

IMN
  amoxicillin rash
  >= 10% atypical lymphocytes

Sepsis
  noradrenaline -> vasopressin
  lactate < 2

Needle-stick
  HBV 33% > HCV 3% > HIV 0.3%
