CARDIOLOGY RAPID REVIEW
=======================


1. HYPERTENSION
---
DIAGNOSIS

Office BP >= 140/90
  |
  v
Confirm out-of-office (preferred)
  |
  +-- Home >= 135/85 -> HTN
  |
  +-- 24h ABPM >= 130/80 -> HTN
  |     day   >= 135/85
  |     night >= 120/70
  |
  +-- Clinic high, but home <135/85
  |   or ABPM <130/80
  |     -> WHITE-COAT
  |
  +-- Clinic <140/90, but home
      >=135/85 or ABPM >=130/80
        -> MASKED
        (suspect if organ damage)

Elevated BP (2024 ESC, new):
  office 120-139 / 70-89
ABPM = best CV prognosis predictor
Orthostatic hypotension:
  drop >20 sys or >10 dia
  (check at 1 and 3 min standing)
Arm diff >10 sys -> use higher arm
---
SECONDARY HTN

Suspect if:
  young onset (<30 y)
  RENAL = most common cause
  |
  +-- HTN + low K -> RAS, Conn's,
  |                  Cushing
  +-- Episodic headache, sweat,
  |   palpitations -> phaeo
  +-- Radio-femoral delay, rib
  |   notching -> coarctation
  +-- Kidney size diff >1.5 cm
      -> RAS
---
DRUG TREATMENT

2 drugs: A + C (or A + D)
  |  not controlled
  v
A + C + D
  |  not controlled
  v
add SPIRONOLACTONE
  (= resistant HTN pathway)

Never ACEI + ARB
Monotherapy only if:
  SBP <150 + low CV risk
  high-normal BP + very high risk
  very elderly
Beta-blocker only if: angina,
  post-MI, HF, rate control
ACEI cough ~15% -> switch to ARB
Isolated systolic HTN -> CCB
TARGET <130/80
  preferred 120-129 / 70-79
Salt <5 g/day; exercise
  >=150 min/week
---
RESISTANT HTN

Target not reached on 3 drugs
(incl diuretic)
  |
  v
Commonest pseudo-cause:
  POOR ADHERENCE
  |
  v
Look for secondary cause
  |
  v
Add spironolactone
---
CRISES

BP >180/110
  |
  +-- acute organ damage
  |     -> EMERGENCY
  |     IV: nitroprusside, GTN,
  |         labetalol
  |     MAP down 25% or DBP
  |     100-110 in 1-2 h
  |     Encephalopathy/ICH:
  |       nitroprusside
  |
  +-- no organ damage
        -> URGENCY
        oral; MAP down 25%
        over hours to days

MAP = DBP + 1/3 (SBP - DBP)
PP  = SBP - DBP
---
PREGNANCY HTN

Safe: labetalol, ER nifedipine,
      methyldopa
Avoid: ACEI, ARB
---
KEITH-WAGENER-BARKER

Gr 1: thickening, tortuosity,
      silver wiring
Gr 2: + AV nipping
Gr 3: + flame haemorrhage,
        cotton wool spots
Gr 4: + papilloedema
        (malignant HTN)



2. ISCHAEMIC HEART DISEASE / ACS
---
STABLE ANGINA vs ACS

Stable:
  mature plaque, thick cap
  70-80% narrowing
  collaterals present
  pain on exertion only
  relieved by rest / GTN
ACS:
  young plaque, thin cap
  -> rupture -> thrombus
  no collaterals
  pain at rest
  not relieved by rest / GTN
---
ACS PATHWAY

Acute chest pain
(ACS until proven otherwise)
  |
  v
ECG within 10 min of FMC
(first ECG normal -> repeat)
  |
  +-- ST elevation / new LBBB
  |     -> STEMI (100% occlusion)
  |
  +-- no ST elevation
        |
        v
      Troponin
        +-- positive -> NSTEMI
        +-- negative -> Unstable
                        angina

Don't miss: aortic dissection,
oesophageal rupture
(antithrombotics -> fatal bleed)

Immediate drugs:
  aspirin 300 mg
  clopidogrel 300 mg
  atorvastatin 80 mg
  IV morphine ~5 mg
  IV metoclopramide 10 mg
Ticagrelor/prasugrel more
  effective (in Sri Lanka now)
O2 only if SpO2 <90%
---
STEMI ECG

Evolution:
  hyperacute T -> ST up (convex)
  -> Q waves -> T inversion

Criteria (>=2 consecutive leads):
  chest leads: >=2 small squares
  limb leads:  >=1 small square

Leads -> artery:
  V1-V4      antero-septal -> LAD
  II,III,aVF inferior -> RCA
  I, aVL     lateral -> LCx
  tall R + ST down V1-V2
             posterior -> LCx/RCA
             confirm V7-V9 ST up
  ST up aVR + widespread ST down
             left main/prox LAD

STEMI equivalent (aVR up + down
elsewhere) -> urgent angio + PCI,
NOT lysis
Isolated ST up aVR -> not lysis
Posterior MI: 15-20% of STEMIs
  needs emergency reperfusion

Other ST up causes: Prinzmetal,
  pericarditis, early repol, LBBB,
  LVH, LV aneurysm, raised ICP,
  V pacing, Takotsubo
---
REPERFUSION

STEMI
  |
  v
Can PCI be done in <=120 min
from FMC?
  |
  +-- YES -> PRIMARY PCI
  |
  +-- NO  -> FIBRINOLYSIS
        (onset <12 h, ST changes
         present; needle <30 min,
         ideally <10)
        |
        v
      ECG at 60-90 min
        +-- >=50% ST resolution
        |     -> success
        +-- failed -> RESCUE PCI

Raised troponin alone is NOT an
indication for lysis
Reperfusion arrhythmias = good sign
---
FIBRINOLYTICS

Tenecteplase (preferred,
  fibrin-specific), bolus by wt:
  <60 kg    30 mg
  60-69 kg  35 mg
  70-79 kg  40 mg
  80-89 kg  45 mg
  >=90 kg   50 mg
Streptokinase:
  1.5 million units in 100 mL NS
  IV over 1 h
  non-fibrin-specific
  antibodies with repeat use

Contraindications:
  prior intracranial haemorrhage
  ischaemic stroke in last 3 mo
  structural cerebral vascular
    lesion (e.g. AVM)
  malignant intracranial tumour
  aortic dissection
  active bleeding

SK hypotension:
  mild (140/90 -> 105/65)
    -> foot of bed up, slow drip
  severe (-> 70/50)
    -> STOP, saline bolus,
       inotropes
---
NSTEMI / UA

NO thrombolysis
Enoxaparin 1 mg/kg SC BD
  NSTEMI: 72 h
  UA: 48-72 h
Enoxaparin > heparin
  (lower HIT risk)
DAPT: aspirin + clopidogrel
---
POST-MI DRUGS

Aspirin 75-100 mg lifelong
Clopidogrel 75 mg >=1 year
Beta-blocker (HR >=60,
  SBP >=100); avoid propranolol
ACEI
Statin even if LDL normal
LDL target <70 mg/dL
  (aggressive <50-55)
No routine antiarrhythmics
---
BIOMARKERS

Marker       Rise    Peak  Normal
Troponin
 conv        ~6 h    n/s   ~10 d
 hs          ~2 h    n/s   -
CK-MB        4-6 h   12-24h 72 h
Myoglobin    1-3 h   6-9 h 24-36 h

hs-TnI: on arrival, repeat 1 h
  (or 2 h)
Troponin also up in CKD,
  non-cardiac surgery
REINFARCTION -> use CK-MB
---
COMPLICATIONS

Post-MI
  |
  +-- Arrhythmia
  |     ventricular = commonest
  |     VF = major cause of death
  +-- Inferior MI -> heart block
  |     atropine up to 3 mg
  |     -> temporary pacemaker
  +-- Papillary muscle rupture
  |     (2-7 d) -> MR
  +-- Pericarditis
  |     fibrinous 1-4 days
  |     Dressler: weeks-months
  +-- LV aneurysm
  |     persistent ST up + Q
  |     -> mural thrombus
  +-- RV infarct (inferior MI)
        low BP + high JVP
        + clear lungs
        -> V3R/V4R -> IV fluids

ACS + HF/shock -> urgent
  angio + PCI
Stunning: recovers spontaneously
Hibernating: recovers with
  timely revascularisation
---
STABLE ANGINA

Antianginals:
  nitrates (GTN mainly down
    preload; no PDE5 inhibitors)
  beta-blocker
  CCB
NEVER beta-blocker + non-DHP CCB
Aspirin reduces events
Triple vessel / diabetic /
  left main -> CABG preferred
Prinzmetal: transient ST up
  -> CCB + nitrates
Stress test:
  horizontal/down ST down =
    ischaemia
  upsloping ST down can be normal



3. HEART FAILURE
---
CLASSIFICATION

HFrEF   EF <40%
HFmrEF  EF 40-49%
HFpEF   EF >=50%

Left HF: dyspnoea, orthopnoea,
  PND, crepitations
Right HF: high JVP, tender
  hepatomegaly, ascites, oedema
S3 = classic sign
Commonest cause of death:
  arrhythmias
Cor pulmonale: RAD, RVH +/- RBBB
  CO2 retention -> warm
  peripheries, bounding pulse
---
DIAGNOSIS

BNP / NT-proBNP (preferred)
  >300; >1000 in acute HF
  best test: HF vs asthma
CXR progression:
  upper lobe diversion
  -> Kerley B
  -> bat wing
  -> snowstorm
---
ACUTE PULMONARY OEDEMA

Sit upright; O2 if SpO2 <90%
  |
  v
IV furosemide 40-120 mg
(rapid venodilation)
  |  still dyspnoeic
  v
IV GTN (if BP normal/high)
  |  not responding, falling
  v  sats
CPAP / BiPAP

Cardiogenic shock (low BP):
  HOLD furosemide + GTN
  -> dobutamine
---
CHRONIC HFrEF

ACEI (or ARNI) + beta-blocker
(carvedilol, bisoprolol,
 metoprolol)
  |  uptitrate to max tolerated
  v
still symptomatic + EF <=35%
  -> add SPIRONOLACTONE
  |
  v
still symptomatic + EF <35%
  +-- ivabradine (sinus rhythm,
  |    HR >70)
  +-- CRT (sinus rhythm,
  |    QRS >130 ms, esp LBBB)
  +-- ARNI

No mortality benefit:
  furosemide, digoxin
Digoxin: HF + AF
Avoid: NSAIDs, verapamil
High-output HF: anaemia,
  thyrotoxicosis, wet beriberi,
  Paget's, AV malformation,
  pregnancy, septic shock



4. VALVULAR DISEASE & CARDIOMYOPATHY
---
MURMURS

MS
  mid-diastolic rumble
  + presystolic accentuation
  loud S1, opening snap
  tapping apex, not displaced
  almost always rheumatic
  ECG: P mitrale
MR
  pansystolic, apex -> axilla
  apex displaced, hyperdynamic
  repair > replacement
AS
  ejection systolic, aortic
  area -> carotids
  slow-rising pulse
  heaving apex, not displaced
  triad: dyspnoea, syncope,
         angina
AR
  early diastolic, LLSE
  leaning forward, expiration
  collapsing pulse
  Austin Flint (apex)
  Marfan, syphilis
TR
  pansystolic, LLSE
  up on inspiration
  CV waves, pulsatile liver
  IVDU endocarditis
MVP
  mid-systolic click
  + late systolic murmur
  Marfan
---
MS DETAILS

Normal valve area 4-6 cm2
AF -> presystolic accentuation
  lost
Calcified valve -> opening snap
  lost
PTMC contraindicated if:
  significant MR
  calcified valve
  LA thrombus
Graham Steell = pulmonary
  regurgitation (pulm HTN)
Lutembacher = MS + ASD
Pregnancy: avoid ergometrine
---
AS DETAILS

Severe AS:
  valve area <1 cm2
  narrow / reverse split S2
  narrow pulse pressure, S4
  -> AVR if symptomatic + severe
Aortic sclerosis:
  no carotid radiation
  normal pulse volume
Pulsus bisferiens = AS + AR
---
S2 SPLITTING

Wide        RBBB, ASD, pulm
            stenosis (delayed P2)
Wide fixed  ASD
Narrow      AS (delayed A2)
Reverse     severe AS (A2 after P2)
---
MANOEUVRES

            Right  Left   HOCM
Inspiration  up    down    -
Expiration   down  up      -
Valsalva     down  down    UP
                   (incl AS)
---
CARDIOMYOPATHY

Dilated: alcoholics; chronic MR
HOCM: S4, jerky pulse, double
  apex, sudden death risk
  -> beta-blockers
Restrictive: amyloidosis,
  sarcoidosis



5. CONGENITAL HEART DISEASE
---
LESIONS

VSD
  pansystolic LLSE
  no increase on inspiration
  highest IE risk
ASD (secundum ~80%)
  wide fixed split S2
  RAD + incomplete RBBB
TOF
  PS + RVH + VSD + overriding
  aorta
  boot-shaped heart, soft P2
  tet spell -> knee-chest
Eisenmenger
  shunt reversal
  loud P2, NO defect murmur
  differential cyanosis (PDA)
  pregnancy = absolute CI
TGA   -> egg on a string
TAPVR -> snowman
Coarctation
  rib notching, figure-of-3
  radio-femoral delay; Turner
Ebstein
  low tricuspid valve
  wide-split S1, TR

Paediatric murmurs:
  ejection systolic: above
    nipple line
  pansystolic: below nipple line



6. ATRIAL FIBRILLATION
---
FEATURES

Atria ~300/min
No P waves
Irregularly irregular
Pulse deficit
Absent A wave in JVP
Types:
  paroxysmal  <7 d (usually
              <48 h)
  persistent  >7 d
  permanent
---
STRATEGY

Rate control
  (>65 y, IHD)
  non-DHP CCB or beta-blocker
  digoxin if HF
Rhythm control
  (<65 y, symptomatic, first
   episode, HF)
  DC cardioversion
  amiodarone, flecainide,
  sotalol

Onset >48 h or unknown
  |
  v
Anticoagulate 3 weeks
  OR TEE to exclude thrombus
  |
  v
Cardiovert
---
ANTICOAGULATION

AF
  |
  +-- Valvular
  |   (rheumatic MS or
  |    prosthetic valve)
  |     -> WARFARIN
  |     AS + AF is NOT valvular
  |
  +-- Non-valvular
        -> CHA2DS2-VASc
        male   >=2 treat; 1 consider
        female >=3 treat; 2 consider
        DOACs preferred

CHA2DS2-VASc points:
  1 each: CHF, HTN, diabetes,
    vascular disease, age 65-74,
    female
  2 each: age >=75, prior
    stroke/TIA



7. CONDUCTION, ECG & ARRHYTHMIA
---
HEART BLOCK

1st      PR >200 ms
Mobitz I progressive PR
         lengthening -> dropped QRS
Mobitz II sudden dropped QRS
3rd      complete AV dissociation
         cannon A waves
         variable S1
---
BUNDLE BRANCH BLOCK

LBBB = WiLLiaM
  W in V1, M in V6
RBBB = MaRRoW
  M in V1, W in V6
---
HYPERTROPHY

LVH: systemic HTN
  S(V1) + R(V6) > 35 mm
  QRS voltage up, duration
  unchanged
RVH: pulmonary HTN
  tall R V1
  T inversion V1-V2
  deep S V6, RAD
---
QT / PRE-EXCITATION

QT normal 0.4-0.44 s
Low Mg -> long QT -> VF
WPW: short PR + delta wave
Brugada: RBBB-like ST -> ICD
---
SVT

Regular narrow-complex
tachycardia
  |
  v
Stable?
  +-- NO -> synchronised
  |         cardioversion
  +-- YES
        |
        v
      Valsalva
        |  fails
        v
      Carotid massage
      (right first, 5 sec)
        |  fails
        v
      Adenosine IV
      6 -> 12 -> 12 mg
      (1-2 min apart)

Asthma -> IV verapamil
  instead of adenosine
---
BROAD COMPLEX TACHYCARDIA

QRS >120 ms, HR >100
VT favoured by:
  QRS >160 ms
  extreme axis
  AV dissociation
  capture / fusion beats
Unstable -> DC cardioversion
---
ALS

Compression:ventilation = 30:2
  |
  +-- Shockable
  |     amiodarone 300 mg after
  |     3rd shock, 150 mg
  |     after 5th
  +-- Non-shockable
        adrenaline ASAP, then
        every 3-5 min
Reversible causes:
  4H: hypoxia, hypovolaemia,
      hypo/hyperK, hypothermia
  4T: thrombosis, tension
      pneumothorax, tamponade,
      toxins
---
JVP

Absent Y descent -> tamponade
Steep X and Y -> constriction
Non-pulsatile raised -> SVC
  obstruction
Exertional LOC: ECG first
  -> if normal, 2D echo
  (e.g. HOCM)



8. INFECTIVE ENDOCARDITIS
---
DUKE (DEFINITE)

2 major
OR 1 major + 3 minor
OR 5 minor

Major:
  blood cultures
    typical organism x2
    OR >=2 positive >12 h apart
    OR majority of >=4 (first
       and last >=1 h apart)
    Coxiella phase I IgG >1:800
  echo
    vegetation, abscess, new
    prosthetic dehiscence,
    new regurgitation
Minor:
  predisposition
  fever >=38 C
  vascular phenomena
  immunological phenomena
  microbiology not meeting major
---
ORGANISMS

Strep viridans
  most common overall; dental
Staph aureus
  increasing; lines, dialysis,
  IVDU (tricuspid)
Strep bovis -> bowel cancer
Enterococcus -> GU/GI
Prosthetic valve:
  early (<=6 wk): S. aureus,
    S. epidermidis
  late: Strep viridans
---
CLINICAL

Triad: fever, murmur, stroke
Osler (tender, finger pads)
Janeway (palms/soles)
Roth spots, splinters,
clubbing, splenomegaly
GN -> low complement
---
INVESTIGATE & TREAT

3 blood culture sets, 3 sites
  BEFORE antibiotics
  (positive ~90%)
  not very ill -> can wait
  2-3 days
TEE = best
  |
  v
IV antibiotics 4-6 weeks
  standard:   benzylpenicillin
              + gentamicin
  MRSA:       vancomycin
              + gentamicin
              (red man syndrome)
  MSSA:       flucloxacillin
              + gentamicin
  Enterococcus: amoxicillin or
              ampicillin
              + gentamicin
Gentamicin: single daily dose;
  hydrate; levels not routine
  in Sri Lanka
---
SURGERY

Severe HF
Failed medical treatment
Large vegetation + embolisation
Abscess / heart block
Persistent fever
---
PROPHYLAXIS

High-risk only:
  unrepaired cyanotic CHD
  prosthetic valve
  previous IE
  cardiac transplant
Amoxicillin 2 g oral, 1 h before
Penicillin allergy:
  clindamycin 600 mg
  or azithromycin 500 mg



9. RHEUMATIC FEVER
---
PATHOGENESIS

Group A Strep pharyngitis
  |
  v
Molecular mimicry
(Type II hypersensitivity)
  |
  v
"Licks the joints,
 bites the heart"
Commonest sequela: MS
Subcutaneous nodules ->
  more severe cardiac disease
---
JONES 2015

Evidence of GAS infection
PLUS 2 major
 OR 1 major + 2 minor

                Low-risk  High-risk
                          (Sri Lanka)
Arthritis       poly only mono/poly
                          or polyarthralgia
Fever           >=38.5 C  >=38 C
ESR             >=60      >=30
CRP             >=3.0     >=3.0 mg/dL
---
TREATMENT

Acute attack
  NSAID / aspirin (taper slowly)
  steroids only if severe
    carditis or carditis + CHF
  chorea: valproate or
    phenobarbital
---
SECONDARY PROPHYLAXIS

Benzathine penicillin
1.2 million units IM q3 weeks

Duration:
  carditis + significant
    residual disease
    -> min 10 y or until 40-45
       (often lifelong)
  carditis + no/mild residual
    -> 10 y or until 25
       (whichever longer)
  no carditis
    -> 5 y or until 18-21
       (whichever longer)



10. PERICARDIUM, AORTA & SHOCK
---
PERICARDIAL DISEASE

Pericarditis
  sharp pain, better leaning
  forward
  global concave ST up
  (not aVR, V1)
  PR depression = most specific
Tamponade
  low BP, high JVP, muffled
  sounds, absent Y, pulsus
  paradoxus, low voltage ECG
  -> pericardiocentesis
Constrictive
  prominent Y descent
  normal echo/CXR possible
---
AORTIC DISSECTION

Tearing pain -> back
Unequal arm BP
  |
  v
Stanford
  A (ascending) -> surgery
  B (descending) -> conservative
IV beta-blocker for immediate
  BP control
---
OTHER

Absent unilateral radial pulse:
  Takayasu, atherosclerosis,
  dissection, subclavian steal
Shock:
  cardiogenic -> cold
  septic -> warm
Blood loss (early):
  diastolic BP up ~10%
  HR up ~20%
  systolic BP down ~30%
Kawasaki: coronary arteritis
  -> IVIG



11. CASE EXAMPLES
---
Prisoner, fever, lung abscesses,
pansystolic murmur LSE
  -> right-sided IE
     (TR -> septic emboli)

Stroke + low fever + apical
mid-diastolic murmur + LA mass
  -> atrial myxoma (mimics MS)

MS + AF on warfarin, INR 0.9,
stroke
  -> embolic stroke
Mechanical valve INR 2-3

BP 220/120, palpitations, sweat
  -> phaeo (MEN2A)

On amlodipine, BP 170, ankle
swelling
  -> add hydrochlorothiazide

BP 230/130 + MAHA, scleroderma
  -> ACE inhibitor



12. HIGH-YIELD POINTS
---
BP cut-offs: 140/90 office,
  135/85 home, 130/80 ABPM
HTN: 2 drugs (ACEI/ARB + CCB)
  -> thiazide -> spironolactone
STEMI: ECG 10 min; PCI if
  <=120 min else lysis (<12 h)
Lysis CI: ischaemic stroke
  within 3 months
aVR equivalent -> PCI, not lysis
Reinfarction -> CK-MB
RV infarct -> fluids
HFrEF: ACEI + BB -> MRA (EF
  <=35%) -> ivabradine/CRT/ARNI
MS: AF abolishes presystolic
  accentuation
Valsalva: all down except HOCM
IE: cultures first; TEE best
RF: benzathine pen 1.2 MU q3 wk
AF: valvular -> warfarin
  non-valvular -> CHA2DS2-VASc
