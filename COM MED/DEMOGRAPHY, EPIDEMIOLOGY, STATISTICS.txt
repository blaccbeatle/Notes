EPIDEMIOLOGY, DEMOGRAPHY & STATISTICS
=====================================



1. VARIABLES & SCALES

VARIABLE
  |
  +-> QUALITATIVE (categorical)
  |     words, no natural order
  |     e.g. sex, religion, blood group
  |
  +-> QUANTITATIVE (numerical)
        |
        +-> DISCRETE
        |     whole numbers only
        |     e.g. siblings, family size
        |
        +-> CONTINUOUS
              fractions possible
              e.g. weight, PEFR, age
              displayed via histogram

---

NOMINAL
  qualitative, no order
  e.g. religion, blood group
  |
  v
ORDINAL
  qualitative, ordered, unequal gaps
  e.g. social class, TNM staging
  |
  v
INTERVAL
  quantitative, equal intervals
  relative zero (can be negative)
  e.g. temperature C / F
  |
  v
RATIO
  quantitative, true absolute zero
  e.g. weight, height, Kelvin



2. CENTRAL TENDENCY & DISPERSION

CENTRAL TENDENCY
  |
  +-> MODE
  |     most frequent value
  |     = peak of curve
  |
  +-> MEDIAN
  |     middle value
  |     even n -> avg of 2 middle
  |
  +-> MEAN
        most common measure
        sensitive to outliers

---

RANGE -> max - min
IQR   -> Q3 - Q1 (middle 50%)

MEAN DEVIATION
  sum of deviations always 0
  -> useless
  |
  v  square the deviations
VARIANCE
  removes cancellation
  units squared
  |
  v  square root
STANDARD DEVIATION
  most common spread measure
  same units as data
  sensitive to outliers

---

n - 1 denominator
  -> degree of freedom
  -> last value fixed once mean known
  -> unbiased sample estimate

---

FORMULAS
  mean     = sum(x) / n
  variance = sum((x - mean)^2) / (n - 1)
  SD       = sqrt(variance)
  z        = (x - mean) / SD
  IQR      = Q3 - Q1



3. NORMAL DISTRIBUTION

NORMAL CURVE
  bell-shaped, symmetrical
  unimodal
  mean = median = mode
  area under curve = 100%
  tails never touch x-axis
  note: symmetrical != always unimodal

---

EMPIRICAL RULE
  +/- 1 SD -> ~68%
  +/- 2 SD -> ~95%
  +/- 3 SD -> ~99.7%
  within +/-1 SD -> also within +/-2 SD

---

STANDARD NORMAL (from z-scores)
  mean = median = mode = 0
  SD = 1
  z = 0 -> 50th percentile
  z = 2 -> top ~2.5%
  A/L exact score
    -> average of 3 subject z-scores

---

SKEW
  right skew -> positive
  left skew  -> negative
  mean, median, mode separate
  mean shifts furthest toward skew

---

SD vs SE
  mean +/- 2 SD
    -> middle 95% of individual values
  mean +/- 2 SE
    -> 95% confidence interval
  SE -> error from sampling
  outside 95% -> "not normal"
              -> not "abnormal"

---

P <= 0.05 -> statistically significant
RELIABILITY
  -> repeated measures, same result
VALIDITY
  -> measures what it is meant to

UNIVERSAL POPULATION
  |
  v
TARGET / STUDY POPULATION
  |
  v
SAMPLE

---

SAMPLING FRAME
  list of all individuals in target
  population (not just size)

---

SAMPLING
  |
  +-> PROBABILITY
  |     SIMPLE RANDOM
  |       lottery, equal chance
  |       most commonly used
  |     SYSTEMATIC
  |       500 / 100 -> every 5th
  |       random start
  |       use when total size unknown
  |     CLUSTER
  |       large dispersed populations
  |       more cost-effective
  |       many small > few large
  |     QUOTA
  |       fixed quotas
  |       ignores real ratio
  |
  +-> NON-PROBABILITY
        CONVENIENCE
          easiest people
        PURPOSIVE (judgmental)
          researcher's judgment
        STRATIFIED
          strata, proportional
        SNOWBALL (= chain)
          hard-to-reach groups
          e.g. drug addicts, sex workers
          cannot combine with
          simple random

---

MULTI-STAGE -> 2+ methods combined
VALID COMBINATIONS
  cluster + simple random
  stratified + simple random

9. PREVALENCE, INCIDENCE & BURDEN

FORMULAS
  prevalence = existing cases
               / population at risk
  incidence  = new cases
               / population at risk
               (time period)
  CFR        = deaths / cases x 100
  DALY       = YLL + YLD

---

POINT PREVALENCE -> one moment
PERIOD PREVALENCE -> time period
  -> useful for mental illness

EXAMPLE (100 workers)
  Feb: 20 anaemic -> prevalence 20%
  May: 30 anaemic (10 new)
       -> prevalence 30%
       -> incidence 10%

NEW CASES -> surveillance or cohort

---

PREVALENCE UP
  in-migration of diseased
  better treatment
  longer disease duration

PREVALENCE DOWN
  out-migration of diseased
  in-migration of healthy
  better screening
  high case-fatality

---

CFR -> SEVERITY, not burden
  generally < 1%
  Sri Lanka ~0.06%
  rabies ~100%
  deaths x2 but cases x4 -> CFR falls

DISEASE-SPECIFIC DEATH RATE -> BURDEN
CRUDE DEATH RATE
  -> not disease-specific burden
DALY -> best overall burden measure
  YLL = years of life lost
  YLD = years LIVED with disability

HEALTH NEEDS
  -> CD incidence + IMR
  -> poverty, not unemployment alone

---

UNITS
  small population -> per 1,000
  MOH area         -> per 10,000
  whole country    -> per 100,000



10. STUDY DESIGNS

STUDY DESIGN
  |
  +-> DESCRIPTIVE
  |     CASE REPORT
  |       1 rare patient
  |     CASE SERIES
  |       ~10-15, selected directly
  |       hospital = more severe
  |     CROSS-SECTIONAL
  |       snapshot -> point prevalence
  |       chronic non-fatal, KAP
  |       can compare 2 populations
  |
  +-> ANALYTICAL
  |     case-control, cohort
  |
  +-> EXPERIMENTAL
  |     RCT
  |     randomise before allocation
  |
  +-> QUALITATIVE
        in-depth interview (~45 min)
        focus group (3-5 people)
        observation
        -> transcribe -> analyse

---

BLINDING
  single -> patient
  double -> + doctor
  triple -> + researcher / analyst
PLACEBO EFFECT
  feel better, think got real drug
SAMPLE SIZE CRITICAL
  case-control, cross-sectional, RCT

---

CASE-CONTROL (retrospective)
cases (disease +) vs controls (-)
  |  look back
  v
past exposure
  -> rare DISEASES
  -> smaller sample
  -> recall bias
  -> no prevalence / incidence
  -> ODDS RATIO
controls -> disease-free only
matching -> 1:1 by age & sex
         -> rare diseases 1:4

---

COHORT (prospective)
healthy people
  |  follow forward
  v
exposure -> disease
  -> rare EXPOSURES
  -> larger sample, longer
  -> expensive, loss to follow-up
  -> no recall bias
  -> INCIDENCE
  -> RELATIVE RISK

---

OR = odds of exposure in cases
     / odds of exposure in controls
RR = incidence in exposed
     / incidence in unexposed

OR > 1 -> risk factor
OR = 1 -> no association
OR < 1 -> protective
ATTRIBUTABLE RISK %
  -> preventable if exposure removed
  -> RR 10 -> 90%

---

CONSENT
  research / surgery
    -> written informed
  emergency -> verbal
  routine visit -> implied
CONFOUNDING
  third factor distorts
  exposure-outcome link
RANDOM ERROR -> unavoidable
SYSTEMATIC ERROR -> preventable
  (faulty scale, observer bias)

---

INTERN SCENARIOS
  first stroke patient
    -> NOT case report (not rare)
  group of stroke patients
    -> case series
  Alvarado score variation
    -> cross-sectional
  contraceptive choice
    -> cross-sectional
  IHD + risk factors
    -> case-control



11. SCREENING

apparently healthy person
  |
  v
screening test
  |  positive
  v
diagnostic test
  |
  v
treatment (if available)

---

TYPES
  MASS -> Pap in clinic
  HIGH-RISK / TARGETED
    -> syphilis -> screen HIV
  MULTIPHASIC
    -> Hb at booking + 28 weeks
  OPPORTUNISTIC
    -> father's BP at child's UTI visit
multiphasic / serial
  -> yield up, sensitivity up

---

SCREENING
  asymptomatic, less accurate,
  cheaper, groups, not for treatment
DIAGNOSTIC
  symptomatic / screen +, accurate,
  costly, individual, basis of
  treatment

---

2 x 2 TABLE
            disease +   disease -
  test +       TP          FP
  test -       FN          TN

sensitivity = TP / (TP + FN)
specificity = TN / (TN + FP)
PPV         = TP / (TP + FP)
NPV         = TN / (TN + FN)
FPR         = FP / (FP + TN)

---

high sensitivity
  -> negative result reassuring
low specificity
  -> positive may be false
PPV denominator = screen-positives

15. FERTILITY MEASURES

RATES (x 1000)
  CBR  = live births
         / total population
  GFR  = live births
         / women 15-49
  ASFR = live births in age group
         / women in that age group
  CWR  = children < 5
         / women 15-49

---

CBR -> poor (includes non-
       reproductive people)
GFR -> better than CBR
REPRODUCTIVE AGE
  Sri Lanka 15-49
  most countries 15-44
ASFR
  highest 25-29
  compares countries
  reflects contraceptive use
  denominator = women, not mothers

---

TFR
  sum of ASFRs
  ignores mortality
  Sri Lanka ~2.2 now
  |  daughters only
  v
GRR
  ignores mortality
  |  + mother's mortality
  v
NRR
  NRR = 1 -> replacement

GRR always > NRR
only GRR, NRR use female births

---

REPLACEMENT LEVEL
  group-level, not individual
  TFR 2.1 + NRR 1
PUNCHI PAWULA
  TFR 2.1 + NRR 1 for ~5 decades
  -> growth ceases

---

CHILD-WOMAN RATIO
  numerator -> under-5 survival
  denominator -> fertility



16. MORTALITY MEASURES

up to 21 wk + 6 days
  -> abortion
  |
  v
22 weeks
  -> early fetal death
  -> perinatal period starts
     (or birth weight > 500 g)
  |
  v
28 weeks
  -> late fetal death
  -> stillbirth
  |
  v
birth
  |
  v
0-7 days -> early neonatal
  (perinatal period ends day 7)
  |
  v
8-28 days -> late neonatal
  |
  v
29-365 days -> post-neonatal

INFANT = birth to 1 year

---

RATES (per 1,000)
  CDR = deaths / total population
  SBR = stillbirths (>=28 wk)
        / (stillbirths + live births)
  PMR = fetal deaths (>=22 wk)
        + early neonatal deaths
        / (fetal deaths + live births)
  NMR = deaths <= 28 days
        / live births
  PNMR = deaths 1 month - 1 year
         / live births
  IMR = deaths < 1 year
        / live births
  U5MR = deaths 0-5 years
         / live births
  disease-specific DR
      = deaths from disease
        / total population
        (per 1,000 or 100)

RATIO (per 100,000)
  MMR = maternal deaths
        / live births

---

ONLY SBR + PMR -> stillbirths in
                  denominator
ALL OTHERS -> live births only
no "child mortality rate" -> U5MR
MMR -> ratio, not rate
    -> treat as rate if grouped
PMR -> most important child-death
       indicator; reflects system

---

2 of 3 infant deaths -> neonatal
EARLY NEONATAL CAUSES
  congenital anomalies > prematurity
UNDER-5 CAUSES
  accidents > congenital anomalies
deaths < 1 year -> MOH investigates
CDR -> not calculable from selected
       disease deaths alone



17. POPULATION GROWTH & LIFE TABLES

growth = births - deaths
         + net migration

STATIONARY
  birth rate = death rate
  -> zero growth
STABLE
  constant non-zero birth and
  death rates
  -> constant growth rate

---

LIFE TABLE
  age-specific mortality rates
  closed to migration
  radix 100,000 at birth
  separate males / females
  -> life expectancy
  -> forecast population
